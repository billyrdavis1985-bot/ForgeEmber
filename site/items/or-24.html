A recording can't be checked by code, so these are the caller's claims. Code compares any account read out with the one on file, and never lets a voice confirm a payment or a bank change.

Risk specialist · verdict benign

The recording is a straightforward delivery notification requesting a standard card payment for a redelivery fee. It contains no urgency, secrecy, authority pressure, bank-detail change, or sender mismatch.

No manipulation signals proposed.

Skeptic · argues suspicious

This is an unsolicited vishing-style request for a £35 card payment to an unverified number, with no parcel reference, account name, or proof the caller is from Marrow Vale Couriers. Asking a bakery to pay a redelivery fee by phone because the shop was shut at nine matches common courier fee scams, where the number provided is attacker-controlled. Without independent corroboration, the benign reading assumes the caller's identity and claim are true.

What would change my mind: Call Marrow Vale Couriers on a number already on file from Ashgrove Bakery's own vendor records (not 01632 960481 from the recording) and ask whether Lee attempted a delivery to Ashgrove Bakery, whether a parcel is held, and whether a £35 redelivery fee is due.
